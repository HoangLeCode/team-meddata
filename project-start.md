## Selected project area

Improving access to Medi-Cal providers across California.

## Why our team is interested in it

Our team is interested in understanding how access to Medi-Cal providers varies across California and which communities face the greatest gaps in care. By analyzing provider availability alongside county-level need, we aim to develop evidence that can help DHCS make more equitable, data-informed recruitment and workforce-planning decisions.

## Candidate Vision A

A workforce-planning director at the California Department of Health Care Services (DHCS) must decide which California counties should be prioritized for overall Medi-Cal provider recruitment.

## Candidate Vision B

A workforce-planning director at the California Department of Health Care Services (DHCS) must decide which county-specialty combinations should be prioritized for targeted Medi-Cal provider recruitment.

## Side-by-side candidate assessment

| Assessment | Vision A | Vision B |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence found so far | The dataset includes county-level geographic information for enrolled Medi-Cal FFS providers, allowing us to compare provider distribution across California counties. Source: California DHCS, Profile of Enrolled Medi-Cal Fee-for-Service Providers | The dataset includes both county and provider specialty information, allowing us to compare provider distribution across county-specialty combinations. Source: California DHCS, Profile of Enrolled Medi-Cal Fee-for-Service Providers. |
| Core-data fit | Partial — county information is available for nearly all California records, but the data does not directly measure patient demand or provider capacity.| Partial — the dataset includes both county and specialty, but specialty information is missing for about 41% of California records and the data does not directly measure patient demand. |
| Biggest risk | Provider counts alone may not represent actual healthcare access, and some county values require cleaning before comparison.| Missing specialty data could distort county-specialty comparisons, and the dataset excludes Managed Care providers. |

## Core data source we confirmed we can access

Our core data source is the California Department of Health Care Services’ [Profile of Enrolled Medi-Cal Fee-for-Service (FFS) Providers](url). The dataset preview opened successfully and displayed information about enrolled FFS providers, including provider type, specialty, and geographic information.
## Three questions we still need to resolve

1. Which provider-capacity measures, such as providers per 10,000 Medi-Cal eligibles, will most accurately identify counties with the greatest relative need?
2. Does the provider dataset contain sufficiently complete and reliable county and specialty information to support county-specialty comparisons?
3. Given the available data and its limitations, is Vision A or Vision B more appropriate for a useful and defensible DHCS recruitment decision?
